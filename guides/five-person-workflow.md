# Five-person concert-video workflow

## 1. Choose the format before choosing the model

For the supplied five-person desk-concert setup, [AI Tiny Desk](https://aitinydesk.net/) provides a direct starting point: fixed performer slots, a selected scene and a matching audio reference. For a different stage or an original scene, explore the broader tools in the [resource list](../README.md).

## 2. Map one portrait to one role

Prepare clear, single-person photos with visible eyes and mouth. Keep a written mapping: slot 1 lead singer, slot 2 keyboard player, slots 3–4 backing performers, slot 5 saxophone player. A partial cast should retain its original role numbers.

Cropping can remove distractions, but cannot recover a blurry face. Review every crop before submission. See the [photo guide](https://aitinydesk.net/how-to-make-ai-tiny-desk) and [role-mapping examples](https://aitinydesk.net/prompts).

## 3. Decide what the audio needs to do

A reference-guided performance asks a model to follow a musical example. Adding an authorized recording in an editor is a different operation. Neither automatically guarantees lip synchronization. Confirm that the selected generation workflow supports the reference input you need.

## 4. Review the whole clip

Check each assigned face across cuts, including the backing performers and instrument close-ups. Listen for lead vocals, backing responses, rhythm and missing audio. Higher resolution does not itself repair an incorrect face or timing.

## 5. Finish and share

Trim the approved take, add captions and review the final crop in an editor such as [CapCut](https://www.capcut.com/tools/online-video-editor). Keep the permissions needed for photos, footage and music. Describe realistic synthetic content as AI-generated when publishing it.

This guide is maintained by AI Tiny Desk's operator. It describes a workflow, not a guarantee of output quality.
